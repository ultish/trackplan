Which shape needs what

Ignore the arrows and count. A connected request with N stops and N−1 links is a tree. Every extra link is a loop.

ShapeExampleWhat the solver doesBacktracking
Fan-outOne start, several endsPrune from the ends inward, then pick from the base outward.None
Fan-inSeveral starts, one joinSame pass. The join keeps the overlap of what every branch can reach.None
Split and rejoinOne start, branches meet againPin one stop on the loop, try each of its platforms, run the tree pass for each.Only over the pinned stop’s platforms
Several loopsTwo diamonds in one requestPin one stop per loop, or a stop shared by both when one exists.Multiplies with each pinned stop

Fan-outs and fan-ins can be mixed freely in the same request, as long as no route forks and comes back together. Only loops cost extra, and a request with no loop never searches.

Where this would sit

A proposal on top of the Assembler and Coupler, not something taken from the spec.

What stays

  • Coupler ExpandKey still answers which assets a slot can reach. Its result becomes the slot's open platforms instead of one bound pick.
  • Inspectors still gate a candidate. A failed inspect removes a platform.
  • Lowest g first still chooses among the survivors.

What changes

  • The leg loop becomes a resolver over a DAG: prune from the ends inward, then pick from the base back out.
  • A shape check comes first. A tree needs no search. A loop pins one stop and tries its platforms in turn.
  • Backtrack targets the pinned stop, not the previous leg.
  • UNSAT names the stop that emptied, which feeds the resolve trace.

Still open

  • Checkpoint (C2c): a stop is probably safe to checkpoint once all its neighbours are bound.
  • Time: a shared asset may be wanted in different windows. Is compatibility time-aware?
  • Several loops: one pinned stop may not break them all, and cost multiplies with each.
  • Sticky cache: keyed on the request shape and domains, not a path.

Formal names, for searching later: pruning from the ends inward is directional arc consistency on a tree-structured constraint problem; pinning a stop on a loop is cutset conditioning. This page is a sketch and is not connected to the engine.
